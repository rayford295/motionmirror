STAGE 3 · BUILT FOR REAL GAMEPLAY

Tracks the right athlete

Contested footage breaks naive pose analysis: defenders steal the track, and phantom detections fire fake events. Three layers keep the numbers honest.

01 · TRACK

Primary-athlete lock

Up to three poses are detected per frame; motionmirror.tracking follows the one that continues the main track by hip continuity and body size. Small athletes get a second, zoomed pose pass. Phantom edge detections can never move the anchor.

▼

02 · GATE

Plausibility-gated events

A release must come from a real shooting motion: knee dip first, then both hands together on the ball at chest height or above. A defender reaching up with one arm to contest cannot claim a shot.

▼

03 · MEASURE

Event-window metrics

Every metric is read at a detected phase (a 5-frame median), never averaged over the whole clip, and every rep carries a confidence with the reason when it drops.

▼
BOSS FIGHT · 1V13 → 2

On the 1v1 gameplay session, the release detector alone flagged 3 shots, one of them a defender's contest after the track briefly switched players. The hands-together gate keeps exactly the 2 shots the tracked player takes. Identity can still switch in close contact; appearance-based re-identification is on the roadmap. Run it on your own video in minutes. Install the package, point it at a clip, and get a report with feedback, a skeleton overlay, key poses and a metrics CSV.

Metric definitions and reference ranges live in docs/metrics.md; every CLI flag is in the README.
